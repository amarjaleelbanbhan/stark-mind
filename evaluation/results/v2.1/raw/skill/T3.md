**Prompt:** My cat likes to sit in boxes that are too small for her.

---

This is actually a well-documented, slightly funny bit of cat behavior with a real explanation behind it.

**Why they do it:** Cats are both predators and prey-sized animals, so tight, enclosed spaces help them feel secure — fewer directions something can ambush them from, and better concealment while hunting or resting. There's also a thermoregulation angle: cats are most comfortable around 86°F (30°C) or so, noticeably warmer than humans like it, and curling into a small space reduces their surface area, helping them conserve body heat. A 2014 study found hospital shelter cats given boxes to hide in showed measurably lower stress hormone levels than cats without one.

**Why "too small" specifically:** cats are remarkably good at judging whether their skeleton (which is quite flexible and lacks a rigid collarbone) will fit through or into a space, even when it looks comically undersized to us. It's not that they misjudge the box — they're choosing compression deliberately. The snugness itself seems to be soothing, similar to the calming effect swaddling has on infants or deep-pressure touch has in some anxiety treatments. There's a popular theory this is why cats also flop into sinks, bowls, or taped-out squares on the floor — it's the boundary/pressure that matters, not really "box" as an object.

A neat thing you could try: research on the taped-square phenomenon suggests even a 2D outline (no walls at all) is often enough to attract a cat to sit in it. If you have tape and a bored cat, that's a fun five-minute experiment in whether the effect is about physical enclosure or just a visually defined boundary.
