**Prompt:** I'm exhausted but I keep wondering why I can't sleep when I'm this tired.

---

That's a frustrating paradox — and a pretty common one. Being tired and being *sleepy* aren't the same state, and the mismatch is usually caused by one of a few things:

**What might be happening**

Your body runs sleep on two mostly-separate systems: a buildup of "sleep pressure" (adenosine accumulating the longer you're awake) and your circadian clock (a roughly 24-hour timer tied to light, routine, and body temperature). You can have high sleep pressure — genuinely exhausted — while your circadian clock is still signaling "stay alert," especially if it's not yet your usual bedtime or if light exposure earlier shifted things. The two systems have to line up for sleep to actually kick in.

On top of that, physical tiredness and mental/nervous-system arousal are different. Stress hormones (cortisol, adrenaline) can stay elevated from the day's events even when your muscles and body feel drained — this is the classic "tired but wired" state. Scrolling, work stress, or even just worrying about *not* sleeping can keep this arousal up.

**A few useful angles**
- *Physiological:* core body temperature needs to drop for sleep onset — if you've been in a warm room or exercised late, that delay matters.
- *Behavioral:* if you often lie awake, your brain can start associating "bed" with wakefulness/frustration rather than sleep (a learned feedback loop).
- *Cognitive:* an overactive mind reviewing the day is still "awake" in the way that matters, regardless of body fatigue.

**The deeper pattern:** tiredness is fuel level; sleepiness is the ignition switch. Low fuel doesn't guarantee the engine starts — you need pressure *and* the right timing/state for the switch to flip.

One thing worth noticing tonight: when you lie down, does your mind feel busy/racing, or does your body feel restless/wired? That distinction points to pretty different fixes (racing thoughts vs. physical arousal).
