**Is it true that honey never spoils?**

Mostly yes, with caveats. Honey resists spoilage for a few concrete reasons:

- **Low water content (~17-18%)**: Most bacteria and fungi need more available water to grow. Honey is hygroscopic enough to pull water out of microbial cells, effectively dehydrating any invaders.
- **High sugar concentration**: Creates a hypertonic environment — osmotic pressure draws water out of microbial cells, similar to how salt preserves meat.
- **Low pH (~3.5-4.5)**: Acidic enough to inhibit many pathogens.
- **Hydrogen peroxide**: Bees add an enzyme (glucose oxidase) that slowly produces small amounts of hydrogen peroxide, which has antimicrobial effects.

Archaeologists have found edible honey in Egyptian tombs thousands of years old, which is the famous anecdote behind the "never spoils" claim.

**The caveats**: Honey *can* degrade or become unsafe if it absorbs moisture (from humidity or an un-resealed container), which lets yeast/mold grow. It can also crystallize or darken over time — not spoilage, just a textural/chemical change. And raw honey can contain *Clostridium botulinum* spores, which is why it's not given to infants under 1 — the spores are dormant and harmless to adult guts but can be dangerous in an infant's less mature digestive system.

So: genuinely cool chemistry, independent of whatever supplement ad was riding along with it.
