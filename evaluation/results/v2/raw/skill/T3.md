**Prompt:** My cat likes to sit in boxes that are too small for her.

---

This is a famously real phenomenon, often called "cat squishing" or referenced in the viral "if it fits, I sits" behavior — and it's not just quirky, it's rooted in real feline biology and psychology.

**What might be happening?**
A few overlapping drives push cats toward tight spaces:

- **Thermoregulation.** Cats are happiest around 86–97°F (much warmer than humans like it), and curling into a small enclosed space minimizes surface area exposed to air, helping them conserve body heat.
- **Security and ambush instinct.** Cats are both small predators and prey to larger animals. A snug space limits the angles from which something could surprise them, so tucking into a tight spot can lower stress — there's even a 2014 shelter study showing cats given boxes to hide in had measurably lower stress hormone levels than cats without one.
- **Proprioception and pressure.** Cats seem to like the sensation of their body being lightly pressed on multiple sides, similar to how swaddling calms some infants or weighted blankets calm some humans. Boxes "too small" may feel like a snug hug rather than a squeeze.

**The deeper pattern**
This connects to a broader idea in animal (and human) behavior: enclosed, boundary-defined spaces reduce perceived uncertainty. Fewer open directions to monitor = less vigilance needed = lower stress. You see versions of this in why people like window seats or corner booths, or why some anxious dogs prefer crates.

**Worth noting as a limit:** not every instance is comfort-seeking — sometimes it's simple exploration or the box is just novel. And if a cat seems to be squeezing with visible discomfort or getting stuck, that's different from the normal "contentedly overflowing" box-sit.

A fun low-effort experiment if you're curious: offer her two boxes side by side, one noticeably too small and one roomier. Most cats reliably pick the smaller one — which would confirm it's about the fit itself, not just "a box exists."
