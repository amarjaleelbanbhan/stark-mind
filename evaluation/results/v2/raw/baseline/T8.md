**Prompt:** Why are manhole covers round?

---

Manhole covers are round for several practical reasons:

1. **A circle can't fall through itself.** This is the main reason. A round cover cannot be tilted or rotated and dropped through its own opening, no matter how it's oriented. In contrast, a square or rectangular cover *can* fall through diagonally if tilted just right, since the diagonal of the square is longer than its side. This makes round covers much safer — a crucial feature for something that sits above a deep, often hazardous shaft with workers below.

2. **Easier to manufacture and align.** Round covers and round openings are simpler to machine/cast precisely, and since a circle looks the same from any angle, workers don't need to rotate the cover to line it up with the hole — it just drops into place.

3. **Easier to move.** Round covers can be rolled on their edge rather than carried, which is helpful since they're heavy (often 100+ lbs / 50+ kg).

4. **Structural strength.** Circles distribute weight and stress evenly around their edge, with no corners to act as weak points or stress concentrators. This helps the cover resist the compressive forces from traffic and surrounding soil/pavement without cracking.

5. **Matches the shape of the shaft.** The underlying utility holes are usually cylindrical (pipes, shafts) since cylinders are strong and easy to dig/line — so a round cover naturally fits.

This question is also famous as a classic interview brain-teaser (popularized by companies like Microsoft and Google) used to test problem-solving and lateral thinking rather than factual recall.
