**Prompt:** I'm exhausted but I keep wondering why I can't sleep when I'm this tired.

---

That's a frustrating paradox — being tired enough to want sleep but too "wired" to actually fall into it. There are a few common reasons this happens:

**Physiological reasons**
- **Cortisol/adrenaline spike** — if you're stressed or have been pushing through fatigue, your body can release stress hormones that override tiredness signals, keeping you alert even though you're exhausted.
- **"Tired but wired" state** — overtiredness can actually make it *harder* to fall asleep, not easier. Past a certain point of exhaustion, your nervous system can tip into a hyperaroused state.
- **Screen/light exposure** — blue light late in the day suppresses melatonin, delaying sleep onset regardless of how tired you feel.
- **Caffeine or food timing** — even if you don't feel "wired," caffeine has a long half-life and can interfere with sleep onset hours later.

**Mental/behavioral reasons**
- **Racing thoughts** — the mind often gets *more* active right as the body tries to rest, especially if the day was busy and you haven't had a moment to process it.
- **Performance anxiety about sleep itself** — worrying about *why* you can't sleep can create a feedback loop that keeps you awake.
- **Irregular sleep schedule** — your circadian rhythm might be out of sync with when you're trying to sleep, so your body isn't ready yet even if it's fatigued.

**What sometimes helps in the moment**
- Get out of bed if you've been lying awake >20 minutes — do something boring and dim-lit, then try again.
- Try a brain-dump: jot down whatever's looping in your head so it's "parked" somewhere other than your mind.
- Slow, extended exhale breathing (like 4 seconds in, 6-8 seconds out) can nudge your nervous system out of that wired state.
- Avoid checking the clock — it tends to fuel anxiety about not sleeping.

If this has been happening persistently (not just tonight), it might be worth mentioning to a doctor, since chronic "tired but can't sleep" can be tied to anxiety, delayed sleep phase, or other sleep disorders. But if it's an occasional thing, it's pretty normal and usually resolves once the stress/arousal settles.
