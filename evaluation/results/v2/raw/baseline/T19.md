As for the actual claim, "honey never spoils":

**It's true, with caveats.**

- Honey has very low water content and high acidity, which makes it inhospitable to most bacteria and microorganisms. Archaeologists have found edible honey in ancient Egyptian tombs thousands of years old.
- However, honey *can* degrade in practice:
  - It absorbs moisture from the air over time, which can allow fermentation or yeast growth if it gets too diluted.
  - It crystallizes/solidifies, though this doesn't mean it's spoiled — it can usually be gently reheated to restore its liquid form.
  - It can darken and lose flavor/aroma over very long periods.
  - If contaminated with water or stored improperly, mold or fermentation can occur.
- Commercial honey is sometimes pasteurized and processed, which can slightly affect longevity/quality compared to raw honey, but doesn't make it unsafe.

So: properly stored, sealed honey can last indefinitely and remain safe to eat, even if its texture or flavor changes.
