**Prompt:** Why do we only use 10% of our brains?

---

We don't — that's actually a myth, not a scientific fact. We use virtually all of our brain, just not all of it simultaneously for every single task.

**Where the myth came from**
There's no single confirmed origin, but it's often linked to:
- A misreading of early neuroscience research (sometimes attributed to Karl Lashley's early 20th-century work on rats, or misunderstood statements by William James about "unused potential")
- Self-help and pop-psychology books that used the idea as a motivational hook ("imagine what you could do if you unlocked the rest!")
- Popular media reinforcing it, most famously the 2014 film *Lucy*, which was built around the premise

**Why we know it's false**
- **Brain imaging (fMRI, PET scans)** shows activity throughout the entire brain over a day, even during sleep. Different tasks activate different regions, but no region sits permanently dark and unused.
- **Evolutionary cost**: The brain uses about 20% of the body's energy despite being only ~2% of body weight. Evolution doesn't tend to preserve large amounts of metabolically expensive, functionless tissue.
- **Brain damage evidence**: Damage to almost any area of the brain — even small regions — produces noticeable deficits (speech, memory, movement, vision, personality, etc.). If 90% were unused, damage there should cause no problems, but that's not what's observed.
- **Neuron studies**: Even "quiet" neurons show baseline activity, and unused neural connections tend to get pruned rather than sit idle indefinitely.

**What's actually true**
- At any single moment, not every neuron is firing at once — that's normal and efficient, similar to how not every muscle in your body is contracting at the same time.
- Over the course of a day, virtually all brain areas get used.
- Some regions (like certain parts of the cortex) are less understood than others, which may have fed the "unused" narrative, but "less understood" isn't the same as "unused."

So the real answer is: we don't only use 10% — we use effectively all of it, just not all at once for every task.
