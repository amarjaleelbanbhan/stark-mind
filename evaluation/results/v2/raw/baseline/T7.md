Ha, that's basically Murphy's Law in action — "anything that can go wrong, will go wrong, and at the worst possible time." It's not really a law of physics, more an observation about human psychology and risk:

- **Selective memory**: Things going wrong at convenient times don't register as memorable; it's the failures during a big presentation, a deadline, or a date that stick with you.
- **Stakes amplify perception**: A flat tire on a lazy Sunday is a non-event. The same flat tire 10 minutes before a job interview feels like the universe is against you — same failure, wildly different emotional weight.
- **Compounding pressure**: When you're already stressed or rushed, you have less slack to absorb a problem, so small issues cascade into bigger ones (you're late, so you rush, so you make another mistake...).
- **Systems under load fail more**: There's a kernel of truth too — things like servers, cars, or bodies are often under *more* strain during high-pressure moments (more traffic, more usage, more stress), so failures are genuinely somewhat more likely then, not just perceived that way.
