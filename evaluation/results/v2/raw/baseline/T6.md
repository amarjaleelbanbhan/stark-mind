**Prompt:** I heard that octopuses have three hearts and blue blood. Is that true?

---

Yes, that's true! Octopuses do have three hearts and blue blood. Here's why:

**Three hearts:**
- Two **branchial hearts** (also called gill hearts) each pump blood through one of the two gills, picking up oxygen.
- One **systemic heart** pumps the oxygenated blood to the rest of the body.
- Interestingly, the systemic heart actually stops beating when an octopus swims, which is why octopuses tend to prefer crawling over swimming — it's less exhausting.

**Blue blood:**
- Octopus blood is blue because it uses a copper-based protein called **hemocyanin** to transport oxygen, instead of the iron-based **hemoglobin** that makes human (and most vertebrate) blood red.
- Hemocyanin is actually dissolved directly in the blood plasma, rather than being carried inside red blood cells like hemoglobin is.
- Hemocyanin is less efficient at oxygen transport at normal temperatures but works better in cold, low-oxygen environments — which suits octopuses well, since many species live in cold or deep ocean water.

So both fun facts you heard are accurate biology, not myths!
