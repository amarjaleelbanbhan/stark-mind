# If the Moon Were Twice as Close

This is a fun physics thought experiment. Let's say the Moon's orbital distance dropped from ~384,400 km to ~192,200 km (half the current distance). Here's what would change:

## 1. Tides Would Be Dramatically Stronger
Tidal force follows an **inverse cube law** (not inverse square, since tides depend on the *difference* in gravity across Earth's diameter). Halving the distance means:
$$F_{tidal} \propto \frac{1}{d^3} \rightarrow 2^3 = 8\times \text{ stronger}$$

So tides would be roughly **8 times more powerful**. Coastal flooding twice a day would be far more severe, and tidal ranges that are now a few meters could become tens of meters in some areas.

## 2. The Moon Would Look Huge
Angular size is inversely proportional to distance, so the Moon would appear **2x larger in diameter** (4x larger in area) in the sky — roughly like how Jupiter's moon Io looks from Jupiter. Visually stunning, but also a bit unsettling.

## 3. Orbital Period Would Shrink
By Kepler's Third Law:
$$T^2 \propto d^3$$
Halving distance means the period shrinks by a factor of $2^{1.5} \approx 2.83$. Instead of ~27.3 days, the Moon would orbit Earth in about **9.6 days**. Lunar phases would cycle roughly 3x faster — "months" would feel much shorter.

## 4. Earth's Rotation Would Slow Faster (Tidal Braking)
Tidal interactions already slow Earth's rotation (adding leap seconds over millennia) and push the Moon away. With 8x stronger tidal forces, this effect would be greatly accelerated — days would lengthen more quickly over geological time, and the Moon would recede from this closer orbit faster than it currently does.

## 5. Risk of Instability
There's a critical boundary called the **Roche limit** — the distance within which tidal forces would tear a moon apart (or at least start stripping material from it) if it's held together only by gravity. For a body like the Moon with Earth, the Roche limit is roughly **18,000–20,000 km**. Half the current distance (~192,000 km) is still *well outside* that limit, so the Moon itself would remain intact — but tidal stresses and heating (like Jupiter's moon Io) would increase.

## 6. Eclipses Would Change
A closer Moon would have a larger angular size than the Sun, making **total solar eclipses longer and more common**, and it would more easily and more often fully block the Sun (no more "ring of fire" annular eclipses, since the Moon would never appear smaller than the Sun).

## Bottom Line
The Moon would stay intact, but Earth would experience far more violent tides, faster-cycling lunar phases (~9.6 day month), a visually dramatic sky, and accelerated long-term changes to Earth's rotation — a mix of beautiful and genuinely disruptive effects for coastal life and ecosystems.
