**Prompt:** Quick — why do we yawn?

---

Nobody knows for certain — there's no single accepted answer, but the leading theories are:

1. **Brain cooling** — Yawning draws in cool air and stretches the jaw, increasing blood flow to the skull/face and helping cool the brain down. This is currently the most favored theory; yawning tends to happen more when people are tired or when brain temperature is slightly elevated.

2. **Boosting alertness** — The deep inhale and stretch increase heart rate and blood flow, possibly giving a mild "wake-up" jolt when you're tired or bored. This fits with why we yawn more when sleepy or understimulated.

3. **Oxygen/CO2 regulation** — An older theory suggested yawning helps expel CO2 and bring in oxygen, but studies manipulating air quality haven't strongly supported this, so it's largely fallen out of favor.

4. **Social/communicative signal** — Contagious yawning (seeing someone yawn makes you yawn) suggests a social function, possibly related to empathy or synchronizing alertness/sleep cycles within a group. This is well-documented in humans and some animals (dogs, chimps) but doesn't explain *why* the physical act itself exists.

So the short answer: most likely it's your body's way of regulating brain temperature and arousal level, with a secondary social-contagion layer on top — but it's still an active area of research without full consensus.
