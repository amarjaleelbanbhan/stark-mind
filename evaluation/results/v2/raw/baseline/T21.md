**Phantom Traffic Jams**

Phantom traffic jams occur without any obvious cause (no accident, no lane closure) — they emerge purely from driver behavior. Here's the mechanism:

1. **Small perturbation**: One driver brakes slightly — maybe to check their phone, change lanes, or just react a bit slowly.
2. **Backward propagation**: The driver behind must brake a little *more* to maintain a safe gap (due to human reaction time), and the driver behind *them* brakes even more, amplifying the effect.
3. **Shockwave formation**: This chain reaction travels backward through traffic as a "shockwave" of braking, even though the original disturbance has long since resolved and that initial driver has sped back up.
4. **Analogy to queues**: It's similar to congestion in queueing systems — a small increase in service time or a brief slowdown at one point can cascade backward through the line, creating a persistent bottleneck that outlasts its original cause.

This is why traffic can mysteriously grind to a halt and then release for no apparent reason — it's an emergent property of density and reaction-time dynamics, not a discrete event.
