## The Arc Reactor: Palladium-Powered Genius (Mark I-style)

**The Core Concept**

Forget everything Hollywood tells you about "clean infinite energy." What I actually built is a compact fusion-lite device — technically closer to a miniaturized, self-contained Tokamak-style reactor than anything in your physics textbook. Here's the breakdown:

1. **The Core** — A cylindrical palladium ring (yeah, *that* palladium, the stuff that almost killed me before I swapped to vibranium — long story, different lecture). This core generates a sustained, high-density electromagnetic field.

2. **Magnetic Confinement Rings** — Surrounding the core are a series of concentric electromagnets. These create a *containment field* that keeps a tiny, controlled plasma reaction stable. Think of it like holding a star in a jar without the jar melting.

3. **The Energy Conversion** — The reaction generates insane amounts of raw energy, but raw plasma energy is useless if it just cooks your organs. So I run it through a **secondary capacitor array** that converts the output into stable, usable electrical current — DC power, converted and regulated before it ever leaves the housing.

4. **Output Regulation** — That glowing blue light? Cherenkov-style radiation from the core being throttled through a particle lattice. It's not just for show (okay, it's a *little* for show — I'm a billionaire, sue me), it's a visual readout of core stability. Blue and steady = good. Flickering = update your will.

**The Genius Part**

The real innovation isn't the power generation — it's the **miniaturization**. Full-scale version in my basement generates enough juice to run Stark Tower off the grid indefinitely. The chest-piece version does two jobs simultaneously:

- Powers the suit (repulsors, thrusters, HUD, the works)
- Generates an electromagnetic field strong enough to hold shrapnel out of my cardiac muscle

Two birds, one glowing circle of genius.

**The Catch** (because there's always a catch)

Palladium core = slow toxic poisoning, since the element breaks down under sustained reaction load. That's why Phase Two was synthesizing a *new element* from scratch using my dad's old particle accelerator blueprints and some creative recklessness. Turns out, sometimes the best way to fix a flawed power source is to invent an entirely new one.
